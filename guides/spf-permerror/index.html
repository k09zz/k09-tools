SPF PermError: the 6 causes and how to fix each one

PermError ("permanent error") means a receiving mail server couldn't evaluate your SPF record at all. It isn't a temporary DNS hiccup: it will fail the same way every time until the record is fixed, and most receivers then count SPF as failed for DMARC.

Find your cause in seconds: paste your domain into the free SPF record checker. It spots duplicate SPF records, counts lookups through every include and flags includes that have no SPF record.

1. Two SPF records on the same domain

The most common cause. A domain may have only one TXT record starting with v=spf1. Two records usually appear when a new email tool's setup guide says "add this SPF record" and someone adds it instead of merging.

Fix: merge them into one. Keep a single v=spf1, put every include: and ip4: from both records in it, and end with one ~all or -all.

# Wrong: two records
v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all
v=spf1 include:sendgrid.net ~all

# Right: one merged record
v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com include:sendgrid.net ~all

2. More than 10 DNS lookups

SPF allows at most 10 terms that trigger DNS lookups (include, a, mx, exists, redirect, ptr), including the ones nested inside each include. The eleventh causes PermError.

Fix: remove senders you no longer use, drop unneeded a/mx, or move bulk mail to a subdomain. Full walkthrough: How to fix "SPF too many DNS lookups".

3. More than 2 "void" lookups

A void lookup is an include or a/mx term that points at a name with no DNS answer, often a provider you cancelled whose SPF host no longer exists. RFC 7208 lets receivers return PermError after two of them.

Fix: remove includes for domains that no longer resolve. The checker flags includes whose target has no SPF record.

4. A syntax error

One typo makes the whole record invalid. The usual ones:

  • v=spf1 missing or misspelled (v=spf, v=SPF1 with stray characters before it).
  • A colon where an equals sign belongs, or vice versa: include= instead of include:, redirect: instead of redirect=.
  • An IP range written wrong: ip4:203.0.113.0/33, or an IPv6 address under ip4:.
  • Smart quotes or a line break pasted in from a document.
  • An unknown word such as include:spf.example.com all (missing the ~ or - is fine; misspelling all is not).

Fix: rebuild the record from scratch with the SPF record generator, which only produces valid syntax, and paste the result over the old one.

5. An include that itself has a PermError

If you include: another domain and its SPF is broken, your evaluation fails too. You can't fix someone else's record.

Fix: check that the include value is exactly what the provider documents today (providers rename their SPF hosts), and contact them if their record is broken.

6. A redirect= with no SPF at the target

redirect=_spf.example.com hands evaluation to another domain. If that domain has no SPF record, the result is PermError.

Fix: make sure the target has exactly one valid SPF record, or replace the redirect with an include.

PermError vs. TempError vs. SoftFail

  • PermError: your record is broken. Fix the record.
  • TempError: a DNS server timed out. Usually fixes itself; recheck later.
  • SoftFail / Fail: the record works, but the sending server isn't listed in it. Add the missing sender.

After fixing

  1. Wait for the DNS TTL to pass (often 5 minutes to an hour).
  2. Recheck with the SPF checker until it shows "pass" with no errors.
  3. Check DKIM and DMARC too with the full SPF, DKIM & DMARC checker: Gmail and Yahoo require all three for bulk senders.
